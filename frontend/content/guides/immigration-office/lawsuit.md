The [immigration office](/glossary/Ausländerbehörde) has 3 months to process your [residence permit](/glossary/Aufenthaltstitel), [permanent residence](/glossary/Niederlassungserlaubnis) or citizenship application. If your application takes longer than 3 months, you can sue them.[^11] This is called an *[[Untätigkeitsklage]]*. It sometimes makes them work faster.

This guide explains when it makes sense to sue the *Ausländerbehörde*, and how to do it.

## Before you start

### Does it work?

**In Berlin, it does not help.**[^1]

- The courts are slow
- The LEA doesn't care, and now even ignores court orders.[^15]

### Costs

It costs €700 to €3000.[^16]

[Legal insurance](/glossary/Rechtsschutzversicherung) does not cover immigration issues.[^2] It will not cover the cost of an immigration office lawsuit.

### Timeline

## How to sue the immigration office

### 1. Wait

You can sue the immigration office 3 months after you sent your application, but **you should wait longer**.[^3]

For a citizenship application, wait at least 12 months.

For a permanent residence application, wait at least 9 months.

For other residence permits, wait at least 6 months.

### 2. Send a letter

First, your lawyer sends a scary letter to the *Ausländerbehörde*. The *Ausländerbehörde* wants to avoid a lawsuit, so they process your application faster.

In big cities like Berlin or Munich, this does nothing.[^20] They just ignore those letters.

### 3. File a lawsuit

If the scary letter does not work, you can sue the *Ausländerbehörde*. The legal system is slow, so a lawsuit can take months or years. If you win, the *Ausländerbehörde* must pay for your legal fees. Usually, the *Ausländerbehörde* reacts before your case goes to court, so you must pay your own legal fees.[^12]

### 4. Court reaction

## Does it work?

In other words, you pay a few hundred euros, and things *sometimes* go faster. A lawyer can pressure the *Ausländerbehörde*, but they can't help you skip the queue.[^13]

In other cities, threatening to sue the *Ausländerbehörde* can make things worse. The *Ausländerbehörde* can become very strict, ask for more documents, and make your application even slower.[^14]
