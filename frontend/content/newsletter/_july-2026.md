## What's new in Germany

### Bürgergeld becomes Grundsicherung

*[[Bürgergeld]]*, which replaced *[[Hartz IV]]* in 2023, is now being replaced with *[[Grundsicherung]]*.

This new *Grundsicherung* introduces stricter conditions, less access to job training, lower housing benefits, and harsher punishments for non-cooperation.[^2] This reflects the current administration's position on most topics: *["the way for success... WORK."](https://www.youtube.com/shorts/Vd-RmAy0oMY)*

### End of the Tankrabatt

The *Tankrabatt* has ended on June 30, which means higher gas prices this July.[^1]

### Car insurance update

I have updated my [car insurance guide](/guides/car-insurance-germany) and related pages. There is finally an English-speaking car insurer, and they recognize foreign driving experience. This helps you get much cheaper car insurance.

That was not an option when I [bought a car](/guides/buying-used-car-in-germany) in 2020. Despite driving for 5 years in Canada and riding a motorcycle across 50 countries, I still started from scratch. My €3000 Kangoo cost €800 per year to insure.

## What I learned this month

These foldable locks will not stop a bike thief. At the end of May, someone tried to steal my beloved bike in broad daylight, on a busy intersection, right in front of a *[[Späti]]*. The *Späti* owner shooed them off, but they still mangled the lock.

Personally, I like a combo of cheap lock and bike insurance, to avoid lugging two kilos of hardened steel around.
