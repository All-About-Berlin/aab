## What's new in Berlin

### Local elections

On September 20, Berliners must vote in city and district (*Bezirk*) elections. They will choose their representatives for the next 5 years. These representatives pass laws that affect all of us. German citizens can vote in state and district elections. EU citizens can only vote in district elections. Non-EU citizens can't vote. For the first time, the voting age is 16 years old instead of 18.

## What's new in Germany

### Ukrainians can exchange their driving licence

Ukraine is now in the [Annex 11 countries](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/fev_2010/anlage_11.html). Ukrainians can exchange their driving licence for a German one. No driving lessons or driving exam needed.

Ukrainians can already drive in Germany. Their licence is recognised as long as their refugee status applies. However, having a German driving licence makes it easier to rent a car or use [car sharing services](/guides/public-transit#rent-a-car).

**[How to convert your driving licence ➞](https://allaboutberlin.com/guides/convert-to-german-driving-licence)**

## What I learned this month

### Anmeldung gotcha

You can only use the same Anmeldung/Abmeldung form for members of the same family. If you register friends, room mates or an unmarried couple, you must use separate forms. No one tells you this, and it should not impact the process, but you will get scolded if you do it wrong.

I have updated my guides and form fillers. Thank you for the tip Katie, and best of luck in Australia!
